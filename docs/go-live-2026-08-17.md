# Going live

Decision taken 2026-08-17: switch production to live Stripe. This is the sequence, the
one thing that will break quietly if it is done in the wrong order, and the one gate
that must stay off regardless.

## What "live" actually is here

**It is a credential swap, not a gate rollout.** The `/pay` destination-charge rail is
not gated — it is the legacy path that produced all 267 payment rows, and it runs
today against a test key. Changing `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` to a live key is what makes
payments real. The 17 feature gates are all *dark new code*; none of them is required
for live payments, and turning them on is a separate, later, incremental rollout.

So: **Steps 1–2 below are "going live". Steps 3–4 are optional and can wait.**

## Not a blocker: the platform has no real users

Confirmed by the owner on 2026-08-17: **everything on the platform is demo. No real
payments, no real users, no real contractors.** The database agrees — 264 of 267
payments carry the demo `test_marker`, no unmarked row reads `paid`, no payment carries
a charge id, and all three billing tables are empty.

That removes the thing this section was originally written to prevent. The concern
below is real mechanically, but its consequence — a working contractor suddenly unable
to receive money — cannot happen, because there is no such contractor. **Go live
without waiting on it.** What follows is recorded so it is understood rather than
rediscovered.

## Connected accounts are mode-scoped (understand it, don't wait on it)

A connected account created in test mode **does not exist in live mode**. Production
has three on file, all marked onboarded and none payouts-restricted:

| Connect id | Status |
|---|---|
| `acct_1TuEg3GjJLVfg2pQ` | **proven live** — it is the destination of the `cs_live_…` Session in the adoption ledger, recorded `provider_livemode = true` |
| `acct_1TtEtEPqTgY5Sbcb` | **almost certainly test-mode** — see below |
| `acct_1Tz4L1KFaZ6LmdiP` | **unknown** |

If two of those three are test-mode ids, then after the key flips those two cannot
receive a destination charge: Session creation fails with `No such destination`. Since
all three are demo accounts, the fix is not remediation — it is that **any real
contractor who onboards from here forward will onboard in live mode and get a live
`acct_`, correctly, with no action required.** The stale demo ids simply stop being
usable, which is the right outcome.

`acct_1TuEg3GjJLVfg2pQ` is proven live, so there is already a working destination to
verify Step 2 against. That is all go-live needs.

### A likely answer to an open question, worth one check

The runbook records the provenance of the three `cs_test_…` pointers as *unknown*, and
notes that creating a Session on the sandbox platform `acct_1TtDcKPqCWgR3Ww0` rejects
their recorded destination `acct_1TtEtEPqTgY5Sbcb` with `No such destination`.

That test was against the wrong platform. The simpler explanation fits everything now
known: **`acct_1TtEtEPqTgY5Sbcb` is a test-mode connected account of the *production*
platform**, and those `cs_test_` Sessions were created by production itself while
running a test key. That is consistent with the Production publishable key being
`pk_test_`, with `stripe_livemode` being null on all 267 payments, and with no payment
ever carrying a charge id.

One step confirms it: look for `cs_test_a1aSm45f…` and `acct_1TtEtEPqTgY5Sbcb` in the
**production platform's test mode** Dashboard (`acct_1TuCWJGqh5LFKuTC`, test toggle
on) rather than on the sandbox. If they are there, the provenance question closes.

## Step 0 — gather two things, then go

With no real users there is nothing to protect by waiting, so this is collection
rather than clearance. In the Stripe Dashboard on `acct_1TuCWJGqh5LFKuTC`:

1. **The live signing secret** for `we_1TuE0BGqh5LFKuTCEyt5d4jh`. **Reveal the existing
   one — do not roll it.** Rolling invalidates the secret the 2026-08-17 in-place
   endpoint correction was specifically designed to preserve.
2. **A live secret key** — `sk_live_…`, or a restricted `rk_live_…` covering Checkout,
   PaymentIntents, Charges, Refunds, Disputes and Connect. Not Prices alone.

Worth a glance while there, but not gating anything:

- **Live mode**, Connect → Accounts: which of the three ids above exist, and their
  `charges_enabled` / `payouts_enabled` / `details_submitted`.
- **Test mode**: does `acct_1TtEtEPqTgY5Sbcb` appear there? That confirms or kills the
  provenance hypothesis above and closes a question the runbook records as unknown.
- Developers → Webhooks, **test mode**: does a test endpoint exist? If so it will stop
  receiving anything once the key flips, which is expected and fine.

## Step 1 — the atomic credential swap

These three must change **together, in one Vercel edit, then one redeploy**. Changing
the key without the webhook secret means every live delivery fails signature
verification and Stripe retries against a rejecting endpoint.

| Variable | New value |
|---|---|
| `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` | live `sk_live_…` (a restricted `rk_live_…` is accepted by the code, but it must cover Checkout, PaymentIntents, Charges, Refunds, Disputes and Connect — not just Prices) |
| `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | the signing secret of `we_1TuE0BGqh5LFKuTCEyt5d4jh` |
| the publishable key (`NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE…`) | `pk_live_…` |

Do **not** touch `STRIPE_BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. It belongs to
`/api/stripe/billing/webhook`, whose route is gated off, and the module explicitly
forbids reusing the platform secret for it.

Then redeploy Production.

## Step 2 — verify the rail is live, with the smallest possible real transaction

1. Confirm the deploy is serving and Stripe live-mode API logs now show traffic.
2. Create one real payment on `/pay` against `acct_1TuEg3GjJLVfg2pQ` — the one account
   proven live — for the smallest amount the flow allows. Pay it with a real card.
3. Confirm: the payment row reaches `paid`, `stripe_payment_intent` is bound,
   `paid_at` is set, `platform_fee` is populated in **dollars**, and the webhook
   delivery shows 200 in the Dashboard.
4. Refund it from the Stripe Dashboard and confirm `charge.refunded` lands and the row
   reflects it.

That exercises settlement and the refund path end to end. Both legacy destination
variants have never been run end to end against real captures, which is the standing
gap this closes.

If Step 2 fails, Step 1 is reversible: put the test credentials back and redeploy.
Nothing in the database needs undoing, because a failed live attempt writes the same
rows a failed test attempt would.

## Step 3 — billing Prices (optional, gated, no rush)

Only after Steps 1–2 are healthy:

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| the six `STRIPE_PRICE_*` | the ids in `docs/codex-authed-browser-tasks-2026-08-17.md` |
| `LGQ_STRIPE_BILLING_LIVEMODE` | `1` |

`LGQ_STRIPE_BILLING_LIVEMODE=1` only becomes valid **after** Step 1, because it must
agree with the mode parsed from `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`. Setting it first fails with
`credential_mode_mismatch`. Every consumer is still gated off, so this changes no
behavior on its own.

## Step 4 — gates, one at a time, later

All 17 stay at 0 until someone deliberately turns one on, watches it, and moves to the
next. Two ordering rules carry over and do not expire:

- `LGQ_LEGACY_DESTINATION_CHECKOUT_GENERATION_ENABLED` goes on strictly **before**
  the projection gate, with a drain period.
- **`LGQ_LEGACY_DESTINATION_CHECKOUT_PROJECTION_ENABLED` must not be enabled at all
  yet, in any order, with any drain length.** `classifyLegacyDestinationCheckoutSignedEvent`
  has no production caller — grep returns its definition and its unit test only —
  while the gate unconditionally stands down the route's three write sites. Today it
  is a kill switch, not a switch: every destination payment would stop being settled
  or failed locally, with no write, no SMS, no job-feed event, no invoice
  reconciliation, no throw, no log, and a 200 back to Stripe. It becomes safe only
  once the webhook wiring slice lands and the classifier is actually called.

## What going live does not disturb

- All three billing tables are empty: `billing_events`, `billing_payment_operations`,
  `legacy_destination_checkout_operations`. Nothing to migrate or reconcile.
- 264 of 267 payments are demo-marked. The only unmarked rows are two `requested` and
  one `failed`. **No unmarked row reads `paid`** — there is no real money anywhere in
  the table, which is why the blast radius here is small.
- The demo rows will 404 against live Stripe if any reconciliation worker ever reads
  them by `stripe_payment_intent`. Only two carry one. Those workers are gated off; if
  one is ever enabled, filter on `test_marker` first.

## The one thing worth doing before real users, not before go-live

267 demo payments, 194 of them reading `paid` with `paid_at` timestamps and populated
`platform_fee`, will sit in production looking like settled revenue against Stripe
objects that do not exist. That is harmless while nobody is looking at reports, and it
is genuinely misleading the moment anyone is.

`scripts/remove-demo-data.mjs` exists for this. It is **not** a go-live step — going
live does not make the demo rows worse, and clearing them is destructive and should be
a deliberate, separately-reviewed pass. But it should happen before the first real
contractor onboards, so trailing-volume and fee-bracket maths are not computed over
$2,600 of money nobody ever collected.

The three unmarked rows (`4b86f692` requested, `bf0df2cb` failed, `ba1dcc4d` requested)
are the only ones that would survive a `test_marker` filter, and none of them is
`paid`, so the correct post-cleanup state is a payments table with no settled history
at all.
